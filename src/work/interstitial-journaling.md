<p>Prior to interstitial journaling, my ideas lived in my Notes app, text messages to myself, and attempts to write them down on paper. I began this ‘project of the self’ to be more intentional about documenting my thoughts and holding myself accountable to the goals I was setting.</p>

<p>Rather than waiting until the end of the day to reflect, I could write while a thought was top of mind. {% ext "https://betterhumans.pub/replace-your-to-do-list-with-interstitial-journaling-to-increase-productivity-4e43109d15ef", "Introduced in 2017 by the now-CEO of Medium" %}, this method involves making short, timestamped entries between tasks, though people have since adapted it to their own lives.</p>

<p>Interstitial journaling can be done on paper, but my interpretation is digital and multimedia. I document my findings (screenshots, quotes, articles galore) alongside my own accounts in a diaryesque format. Together, they become a mnemonic device that helps me revisit my thought process.</p>

<p>Over time, this ‘unstructured data’ has become a mood board for future projects. The idea for this website began through these entries, too.</p>

<p>It is also my way of adapting to an age of generated media. Keeping a record of my thoughts helps me return to how an idea developed, and distinguish what is mine from what inspired it. {% work "how-to-cite-ai", "Citation" %} is something I think about now more than ever.</p>
