<p>As a freshman at Syracuse University’s School of Design, I worked as a research assistant alongside {% ext "https://vpa.syracuse.edu/faculty-staff/nyboer-jody-1/", "Jody Nyboer" %}. This study explored college-age students’ workspace preferences and experiences, a perspective that had received limited attention in research on designed work environments.</p>

<p>Specifically, how the a space affects a student’s ability to (1) work effectively and (2) at their best.</p>

<p>I owned the discovery phase, reviewing precedent studies to narrow down our research question and define the scope of the study. I also developed activities for participants to document their work environments and measure phases in their creative productivity, via {% ext "https://arl.human.cornell.edu/linked%20docs/Cultural%20Probes_Gaver.pdf", "cultural probe kits" %}.</p>

<p>Working in design research inspired me to move away from the belief that I had to be a designer to do design. I slowly began to separate the user experience from the interface and think more carefully about design as a series of choices that influence behavior, not just how something looks or feels.</p>
