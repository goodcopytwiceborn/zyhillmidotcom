<p>Created in collaboration with an old classmate, this podcast asks how much of our anxiety about AI-generated art comes from treating art as a commodity…and how those concerns might change if the value of art weren’t measured by its ability to generate income.</p>

<p>I later used an excerpt of this conversation in a {% work "the-gospel-of-tech", "zine" %} I created. The text is reproduced below for readability:</p>

<details class="more">

<div class="dialogue">
<p class="them">“How do you feel about AI art and its implications?”</p>
<p class="me">“If you think about it through the lens of photography, think about stock photos. Do stock photos take away from photography?”</p>
<p class="them">“So you’re saying this art, form of making art, shouldn’t be feared by artists that put artistic intention into their work?”</p>
<p class="me">“AI art was made for convenience. People play around with it, but ultimately it’s for commercialization, not curation.”</p>
<p class="them">“Do you think that anyone would care about the moral implications of AI if we didn’t tie art to our brand and money making possibilities?”</p>
<p class="me">“For whatever reason someone makes art, if we just focused on that and didn’t rely on its economical value would anyone actually care about the existence of AI art?”</p>
